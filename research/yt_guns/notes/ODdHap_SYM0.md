# ODdHap_SYM0 — "How to Create a Tearout/Dubstep Gun Sound!" — Ghosthack (6:06)

Ableton rack; sample-based gun. Good concrete trick: modulated disperser split.

## Transient / source
- Two **percussion samples** (Ghosthack "Desktop Essential Percussions"): one main "that already has some low end", one "just for top end" (could be a clap).
- Main sample **transposed up, +26** semitones "sounds so cool" [captions "on 26"; inferred semitone transpose in Simpler/Sampler]. Changing the sample's transposition = changing the gun's color/note.

## Body
- **Pedal** (Ableton) distortion → compression → more pedal/"heat".
- Ableton-native **disperser rack**: several instances of **EQ Three** (allpass-ish phase via crossovers) with low/high split frequencies mapped to **Macro 1**, and that macro **modulated by a MIDI envelope** per note. "Without the movement the whole sound is very different" — the swept dispersion is key. (Kilohearts Disperser: map its frequency the same way.)

## Reverb
- **Hybrid Reverb** preset "Car Garage" (convolution IR); only size and decay changed; short decay (~RT60 shortish), followed by EQ **boosting ~1.6 kHz** mids "because that is the interest of the sound".

## Sub (separate)
- Analog **sine** ("analog BD sine") with filter crunch, drive inside the synth.
- Two pitch modulations: **pitch envelope at the start** (kick-like punch; Env 2), plus **slow LFO 2 vibrato** on pitch.

## Drums
- Clap transposed **-5 st**, overdrive, mid-boost EQ, reverb; watch level vs kick.
