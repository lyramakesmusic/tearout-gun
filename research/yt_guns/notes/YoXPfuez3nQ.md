# YoXPfuez3nQ — "HOW TO ACTUALLY LAYER YOUR DUBSTEP BASSES" — oddprophet (22:56)

FL + Serum + Multipass. Philosophy: **one powerful bass + many small high-frequency details**, not stacking big basses. (Captions are a machine-translated dub; content still clear.)

## Core gun (quick rebuild)
- WT; **LFO envelope mode** for transient; tempo **140**.
- **Band-pass + notch filter** on the same LFO.
- **LFO2 → master tuning** (pitch).
- Notch filter for dynamics; distortion.
- Multipass: phase distortion (on a resonant freq), **convolver = "the most important part"** made shorter (not "cave"), tone up; OTT (not overdone); **shaper** to clip tops back to loudness.

## Layering rules
- Don't layer in the sub / low-mid (no space; "layering two subs = big problems"). Layer in **upper-mid and high**.
- Layers: his free **"synth noise" pack** (white noise amplified through a subwoofer, re-recorded), **foley** (Mortal Kombat-style: glass ringing/breaking, etc.), reversed samples, pitched up, low-cut, quieter than the gun ("don't drown out the gunshot").
- **Tonal layer**: clone the Serum gun, Multipass with frequency shift of the mids, convolver on LFO level, EQ everything but the needed band out.
- Quarter-note layer with a **1/4 LFO on a frequency shifter** ("woo-woo").
- Group EQ to smooth spectrum (beware phase from many EQs → linear phase).
- Parallel layer through **Dubstepifier** (Freakshow Industries pitch-shifter texture) on a Patcher, sub disconnected/low-cut, blended low.

## Principle
- "Butterfly effect": after heavy processing, swapping the WT changes everything → happy accidents; most WTs crap, some great.
- "Human brain can't perceive 2–3 massive basses at once."
