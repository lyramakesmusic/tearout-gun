# LM5LgEvjNHs — "How to Make HEAVY TEAROUT GUNS Like MARAUDA (2024 Update)" — Dripment (18:51)

Ableton; sample-collage gun, resampled. Strong on **reverb/convolution** and **triplet rhythm**.

## Source / body
- Layer many **bass one-shots** and gun-bass samples; **transpose each to root (D minor here)** in Complex warp mode; use a key-finder; pick samples covering **high/mid/low** ranges; some percussion/claps (untuned) for transients. MIDI-triggered sampler copies of rhythm for extra transient layer. One layer through **Corpus** (wide, no reverb).
- First processing on "Drip Tear Guns" group: **Disperser set high (not low) for a "clean lasery/phasey effect at the beginning of the transient"** → "loud rack": OTT **81%** → Saturator **5 dB** → OTT **76%** → Saturator **6 dB** → EQ8 boosts → Glue comp → Utility → **GClip at 0 dB** w/ gain.

## Reverb / convolution (central claim: Marauda guns have "so much reverb / lots of space")
- Guns group: EQ8 cut sub → **Convolution reverb loaded with one of the tearout bass samples as the IR** ("user tearout bass", **30%** mix) → Multiband Dynamics **33%** (clean gain).
- Individual layers: some with reverb (one at **38%** — "this reverb is doing a lot"), some dry; **4 convolution reverbs total** in the chain. Final reverb on the resample **22%** using a "**kick verb**" convolution.

## Sub
- Separate clean sine following the **same rhythm**; **GClip sub+guns together at +5 dB drive** ("Mixology room") — "clip sub and basses together, that's the method". Final separate sub with GClip **4 dB drive**.

## Resample / final chain
- Resample the gun pattern → EQ8 cut at **100 Hz** → compression → Saturator **4 dB drive** → Multiband Dynamics **44%** → **Pro-L** loud (+4) → reverb **22%** kick-verb convolution → target ~**-4 LUFS** short-term. Lows bus **-3 dB**; master clipper.

## Pattern
- Marauda "Casket VIP" rhythm: **all triplet timing**, grid **1/8T and 1/16T**, "really fast and spaced-out triplets" — 1-2-3 1-2-3 1 1-2-3 groupings rather than filling every triplet; slower triplet section later. 4-bar pattern.
