# SNWU8aghQ40 — Ableton Live Dubstep Tutorial - Filthy & Nasty Bass Processing / Resampling (Part 2)
AscianUK · 13:19 · Ableton (multiband-split resample processing)

**Relevance:** Medium (older-school filthy dubstep, not tearout). Per-band chains in a 3-way split; notch-sweep + saturate-after-notch logic.

## High band (starts ~2.5 kHz)
Saturation → phaser → (chorus/phaser/flanger/delay suggested; "don't compress the highs too much") → Auto Filter ("wow" filter) with LFO matching mids → **EQ Eight notches swept fast up (and another down)** to make a dropout → **saturation again ("notches take out frequencies, saturate after to bring the meat back")** → second filter (high-pass) after saturator ("filters after saturation latch onto new harmonics") → delay → phaser → a bit of reverb → high cut.

## Mid band
Wow filter, Camel (CamelCrusher fat), flanger, overdrive, notches → compressor to bring level up → **surgical EQ: max Q, max gain sweep-and-cut ugly resonances**; cut **~200 Hz** (snare space); harsh cut below **315 Hz** (crossover to bass band).

## Low band
Below ~315 Hz [inferred crossover], handled separately (Part 1).

## Quotes worth keeping
- "these notches are taking out frequencies it's always good to saturate after just to bring the meat back"
- "valve filters always good after saturation because it creates more harmonics for the vales to sort of latch onto"
- "don't be scared to take a lot of frequencies out ... if you lose power you can use compression"
